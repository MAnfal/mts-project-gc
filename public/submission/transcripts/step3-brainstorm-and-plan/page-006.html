## 2. Patterns that work

  1. Show variance before showing deltas. Mean on the surface, spread or individual repeats one click down [5][1][10]. None of the pages I read showed a noise band on the deltas themselves. For you, that means drawing the A/A swing next to every delta (inferred).
  2. Compare against a baseline you choose, with direction-aware green and red [1][4][12].
  3. Make the regression counts clickable filters [1][4].
  4. Warn when runs aren't comparable (Weave's inconsistency flags) [12].
  5. Keep the hierarchy to three levels: summary, per-example rows, trace in a side panel. Disclose the trace progressively, keeping less important details "in collapsed sections" [14].
  6. Render traces for the domain, put scores and pipeline version inline, show progress ("Trace 45 of 100"), and add hotkeys [14].
  7. Show failure-mode counts from error analysis (axial coding, then count per category) rather than generic scores [17].

Anti-patterns: - Generic metrics are "worse than useless", and "too many metrics fragment your attention" [15]. - "Don't rely on generic evaluation frameworks" [16]. - Reporting raw agreement on imbalanced labels [16]. That's relevant to how you present the judge panel. - Criteria are only partly known before grading ("criteria drift", Shankar et al.) [18].
